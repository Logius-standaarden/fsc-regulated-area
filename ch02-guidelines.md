# Guidelines

There are no hard restrictions on the creation of a Regulated Area withing an FSC Group.
However, there will be some additional maintenance for operating a Regulated Area and the additional restrictions of Peers exchanging data. 
Creating a Regulated Area should be a well considered decision.

This non-normative section offers some guidelines that may aid the decision process whether it is beneficial to create a Regulated Area.

A Regulated Area is a subsection of an FSC Group imposing additional membership restrictions for exchanging data.
Entrance to a Regulated Area and overall membership is governed by one or more Membership Administrators. 
The Membership Administrator applies a set of rules for the entry criteria for participation in the Regulated Area. 
A clear and well-defined set of rules acts as a basis for participation. If such clear entry criteria are not available or differ too much between Peers the creation of a Regulated Area may not be appropriate.

It is expected the members or a Regulated Area have some sort of cohesion as a sub set of all the Peers in the FSC Group. Some examples of such cohesion are:
* There is shared governance, for example a law with accompanying policies requiring collaboration of a subset of Peers within the Group
* part of the same value chain for example employment or law enforcement

A Regulated Area *may* be appropriate if:
* a subset of Peers within an FSC Group that share a need to exchange data based on a common basis or a common set of rules or entry criteria based on the cohesion mentioned above
* a form of overarching governance is needed for this subset of Peers exchanging data
* the same set of rules and guidelines apply to the subset of Peers exchanging data

A Regulated Area *may* not be the right approach if:
* the validation rules of allowing access to data varies between Peers
* there is no need for external governance for data sharing
* it is hard to define the subset of Peers within an FSC Group (there is no common denominator specific enough to define a subgroup)
* the collaboration is temporary, for example a construction project
